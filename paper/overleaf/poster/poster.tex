\blockhead{Why ASCON-128 on Real Hardware?}
ASCON-128 is the NIST SP~800-232 lightweight cryptography standard,
entering constrained FPGA deployments. Its security under
\emph{physical} adversaries --- power analysis and fault injection ---
determines whether the standard's promise holds in the field.
Published FPGA attacks evaluate \emph{custom} ASCON cores (SCARL) or
\emph{software} implementations (ACNS'24 ensemble) --- never the
official unmasked LWC core. We measure that core directly.

\blockhead{Oracle-Verified Testbed}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.96\linewidth]{figs/testbed.pdf}\par
\vspace{4pt}
\begin{itemize}
\item CW305 Artix-7 + CW-Husky, 40\,MS/s vs 10\,MHz crypto clock (4
  samples/cycle), VCCINT shunt, per-query trigger.
\item \textbf{Byte-exact oracle gate:} every stored trace accepted only
  when its ct/tag readback matches a software reference (5/5 NIST
  KATs). Kills misconfigured-target errors before they poison
  datasets.
\item Masked (d=1, 2-share) reference core on the same platform ---
  the unmasked-vs-masked contrast isolates masking as the causal
  variable.
\end{itemize}

\blockhead{DL-SCA Setup: Divide-and-Conquer}
One CNN profile per S-box column: each of the 64 columns carries
\textbf{2 unknown key bits} $\Rightarrow$ 4 hypotheses per column.
Train on 5{,}000 random-key traces (80/20 split), HW labels 0--5.
Random keys are essential: at a fixed key the column label is a
function of two \emph{public} nonce bits (see Gates, trap 3).

% ============================================================
% COLUMN 2 — DL-SCA RESULTS
% ============================================================
\blockhead{Result 1: Every Column Leaks}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{figs/keyrank.pdf}\par
\vspace{4pt}
\begin{itemize}
\item Held-out random-key traces: key-rank top-1 \textbf{42.7\% mean
  vs 25\% chance}; \textbf{all 64 columns} above chance, none below
  30\%. First-order, real, and per-column.
\item Naive single-trace top-1 assembly reaches 37/128 bits ---
  accumulation (below), not single traces, is the recovery path.
\end{itemize}

\blockhead{Result 2: The Profiling-Domain Gap}
Offline profiles transfer poorly to live board sessions ---
unfine-tuned columns score at chance on-board. \textbf{Live
fine-tuning fixes it:} 300 board traces at a known key raise training
accuracy to 90.7\% (vs 72\% majority floor), and the fine-tuned column
recovers \emph{correct} bits at M=1 while unfine-tuned columns sit at
chance.

\blockhead{Result 3: Closed-Loop Recovery + SNR Lever}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{figs/convergence.pdf}\par
\vspace{4pt}
\begin{itemize}
\item Posterior-scoring chosen-nonce attack converges to the correct
  hypothesis in \textbf{32 queries (posterior 0.999)} --- public
  information only.
\item Nonce-repetition averaging: repeating one separating nonce $M$
  times lifts true-hyp rank-1 from \textbf{0.25 (chance) to 0.94} at
  M=64; convergence shortens 15$\rightarrow$9 queries/column.
\item Full 128-bit assembly: divide-and-conquer over columns + bounded
  enumeration of least-confident columns, verified by re-encryption.
  \textbf{Ongoing work} --- the pipeline is complete; the remaining
  gap is on-board fine-tuning budget, not information content.
\end{itemize}

\takeaway{The unmasked NIST core leaks its key bits column-by-column;
$\sqrt{M}$ averaging and on-board fine-tuning are the levers that turn
weak per-trace signal into confident recovery.}

\blockhead{Clock-Glitch Fault Injection}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{figs/glitch_sweep.pdf}\par
\vspace{2pt}
\includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{figs/fault_map.pdf}\par
\vspace{4pt}
\begin{itemize}
\item A stable fault band at $e_o{=}41$ (width 67): 406 band hits over
  153 distinct tags; differentials 27--41 bits.
\item \textbf{12 clean single-bit round-11 faults} localized to exact
  (column, bit) --- 10 distinct state columns --- each verified by
  exact faulty-tag match under simulated single-bit flip.
\item \textbf{Data-selected placement:} each nonce faults exactly one
  column, reproducibly $\Rightarrow$ DFA coverage bounded at one
  column/query. Nonce-set engineering ($\approx$266 nonces,
  coupon-collector bound) restores coverage.
\item Burst glitches: 400/400 faulted but multi-bit (30+ bits) --- not
  composable.
\end{itemize}

\blockhead{Masked Core (d=1, 2-share)}
Same pipeline, 9{,}151 traces: S-box target at \textbf{chance for
every architecture}. First-order masking works; cost $\approx
1.8\times$ throughput, $2.1\times$ LUTs.

\midrule
